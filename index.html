Conference paper: Evaluating the Role of Next-Generation Battery Materials in Improving the Energy Density, Charging Speed and Performance of Electric Vehicle Batteries

Abstract

The future will contain many new materials and devices but how will we charge them, and how will they affect the environment around us? This paper aims to identify the different new materials and to evaluate the efficiency of them so that we can understand which ones are best for us to use to charge electric cars and next-gen tech. Modern lithium-ion batteries have problems such as degradation of the battery, restrictive capacity and the reliance on raw materials which can be dealt with using various methods such as using buffer zones which includes an upper and lower limit to the battery charge but this also means that the battery will not be used to it's full capability. Batteries can be recycled or reused to mitigate raw material mining which not only is innovative but also helps the environment and reduces e-waste. One way that the new-gen will evolve is by using silicone instead of lithium-ion because "silicon electrodes can provide 10 times higher theoretical capacity and faster charging"(QMUL, 2025) However, silicone's volume will change by 300% during cycles which means they will degrade quickly and won’t last long.(QMUL, 2025)

Reliance on Raw Materials

The development of battery technologies have created many opportunities to improve the technosphere used in the car industry however, this development has relied on the extraction and processing of raw materials from the ground which can harm potential farmlands and habitats. Materials such as lithium, nickel and cobalt are all used significantly in the batteries and raw materials that are extracted and as the technologies develop further, acquiring these materials has become a challenge. There are environmental, economic and ethical concerns with mineral mining. "Lithium is typically mined through a process called brine mining, which involves extracting lithium from underground saltwater reserves. The risks in polluting local water sources arise here" (March Zheng, 2023). There are many examples such as the Salar de Atacama in Chile and are a growing concern for the locals living there. The next generation batteries including solid-state, sodium-ion and lithium-sulphur reduce the dependancy on these raw materials and will help but these technologies will also introduce their own limitations and requirements. The solution to reducing material extraction is simple; we would need to invest more in material recycling, figure out different battery chemistries and we would need to change our extraction methods to be more suitable for the environment. These methods are essential and would reduce the need for additional and unnecessary mining and would help establish an appropriate battery chain. The transition to newer technologies would need to consider and balance all factors so that the longevity of the battery and raw materials can be fully accessed and will benefit the world in the long term.
