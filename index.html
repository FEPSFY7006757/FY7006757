Conference paper: Evaluating the Role of Next-Generation Battery Materials in Improving the Energy Density, Charging Speed and Performance of Electric Vehicle Batteries

Abstract

      The future will contain many new materials and devices but how will we charge them, and how will they affect the environment around us?
      This paper aims to identify the different new materials and to evaluate the efficiency of them so that we can understand which ones are best for us to use to charge electric cars and next-gen tech.
      Modern lithium-ion batteries have problems such as degradation of the battery, restrictive capacity and the reliance on raw materials which can be dealt with using various methods such as using buffer zones
      which includes an upper and lower limit to the battery charge but this also means that the battery will not be used to it's full capability.
      Batteries can be recycled or reused to mitigate raw material mining which not only is innovative but also helps the environment and reduces e-waste.
      One way that the new-gen will evolve is by using silicone instead of lithium-ion because "silicon electrodes can provide 10 times higher theoretical capacity and faster charging"(QMUL, 2025) 
      However, silicone's volume will change by 300% during cycles which means they will degrade quickly and won’t last long.(QMUL, 2025)
